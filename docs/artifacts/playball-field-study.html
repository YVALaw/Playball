Part one · The field

There is a real and surprisingly crowded genre here, and almost none of it is baseball. Three shapes recur, and each one is a different bet about what a phone is for.

The direct competitor
College Baseball Simulator

iOS only. Free, 4.8 from 112 ratings, 50.7 MB. Recruiting on a budget, a transfer portal, pitch-by-pitch play with live win probability, depth charts, an ABL draft. $3.99 God Mode; checkpoint saves $0.99–$3.99.

It is one developer's third app in a year, off the same shell as CFB Simulator (4.8/702) and CBB Simulator. The shell is thin — 47–51 MB, English only, iOS 18+ — and the whole differentiation is the sport in the title.

Archetype · native list app
Looks like Settings

System font, grouped inset lists, a bottom tab bar, sheets. Cheap to build, instantly legible, completely interchangeable.

  • Dynasty: College Basketball — 3.8/23
  • College BBALL Coach 2 — 3.7/818
  • College Football: Dynasty Sim — 4.0/590, and it charges $0.99 for dark mode
  • Hardwood Empire, Blue Bloods Basketball
Archetype · game-styled
Looks like a game

Astonishing Baseball Manager: 4.7 from 2,700 ratings, 238 MB, landscape. Dark panels, painted card art, letter-graded attributes as coloured badges, morale and energy meters, player cards as sheets over the standings.

An order of magnitude more ratings than any list app. Its top review is a player shouting that it is not pay to play.

Archetype · arcade hybrid
Puts a moment in the middle

Retro Bowl College, Hoop League Tactics, Full Court Rivals. Strip the sim down, hand the player something to do with a thumb. Retro Bowl's thesis — tap to pass, swipe to juke, light management — made it the most played football game on mobile by a distance.

The gap on the shelf

Nobody has shipped a serious college baseball dynasty on Android. The nearest thing, College Baseball: Dynasty Builder, is Windows-only on Steam (96% positive of 32 reviews) and its pitch is ours: honest recruiting where the AI plays by the player's rules, prestige tied to school attributes, conference tournaments into regionals into supers into a College World Series.

Worth stealing from, outside the sport

  • WrestleVerse sells itself as “the depth of TEW, the accessibility of MyGM, mobile-first”, and its headline feature is an Auto Booker that fills a complete show in seconds. A fast path through the depth rather than instead of it. Best idea in the adjacent market.
  • Football Chairman Pro — seven divisions from nothing, tiny, and the longest-lived mobile management game there is.
  • Basketball Dynasty Manager — markets on “no ads and no in-app purchases” as the feature itself.

Part two · Architecture

The closest sibling is not a phone game

ZenGM — Basketball GM and its siblings — is a single-player sports management game written entirely in client-side JavaScript, and it independently arrived at the architecture Playball already has.

  • The UI runs on the main thread; the game runs in a Worker. Two folders, two globals, messages between them.
  • Data lives in IndexedDB with an in-memory cache on top; the database is only touched for the uncommon case — old seasons, historical stats.
  • The worker is not tidiness. A browser throttles JavaScript in a background tab, so a simulation on the main thread stops when the player looks away.

Playball matches it: simWorker.ts behind Comlink, persistence.ts over idb, and the house rule that the engine imports nothing — enforced by a test. That rule is why the engine could be moved onto a worker at all, and it is worth more on a phone than it ever was in a browser tab.

The one idea not yet taken is the explicit cache tier. Today the store is the cache and it is large. On a phone the question is not how big state is in memory — it is how much crosses the worker boundary and hits IndexedDB per save, and whether that happens while something is animating.

The genre's real failure mode is reliability

Read enough reviews and the same words recur across every app in the field, regardless of engine, price or platform.

AppRatingWhat its reviewers report
OOTP Baseball 27 Go! 4.3 / 46 Crashes in the inaugural draft; “crashed 3 times in less than 3 hours”; an action button broken on the lineup screen for three years.
College Baseball Simulator 4.8 / 112 “Gets stuck with the loading bar” — a reviewer calls it unplayable. Prestige climbs too slowly; a whole roster lost after one down year.
CFB Simulator 4.8 / 702 Interface bugs causing freezes that need a restart; a run of releases fixing save corruption and playoff progression. Accidental swipe-dismiss.

Dynasty: College Basketball 3.8 / 23 Freezing and crashing, losing progress. NIL offers that will not negotiate.

The finding of the whole study

Two apps in this genre sell checkpoint tokens as an in-app purchase — $0.99 for one, $2.99 for five, up to $3.99. Players are paying real money for the right not to lose a dynasty.

Playball has 1,061 tests across 45 files, determinism goldens, calibration as a regression test and a concurrency suite pinning the store's double-press guards. In this market that is not diligence, it is the product. Which sets one hard requirement for the port: IndexedDB writes must survive the process being killed. Android will kill a backgrounded WebView, and a half-written save is precisely the corruption those reviews describe.

  • State is pinned, not scrolled Score, inning, outs and count never leave the screen. Everything else may move under them.
  • The last event is a sentence Not a diff, not a stat line. One line of plain English in its own card. This is the part Playball's stage 15.5 is already built to beat.
  • The decision is a fixed grid Six tiles that never move between pitches, one wearing a Selected badge. Muscle memory beats a menu.
  • The escape hatch is the biggest button Full-width, under the thumb. CFB Simulator goes further and labels its primary “Suggested: Run” — the game telling a lapsed player what a competent coach would do.

Recruiting is a weekly budget, not a menu

The competitor's recruit screen is two list groups and a header. Weekly Actions — maintain contact, call, attend a game, each with its cost and a done state — sit visually apart from One-Time Actions, because they are different kinds of decision. Then the prospect: stars, position, national rank #248, and Interest 34% on a coloured bar, so a whole board is scannable in one pass. Then a plain key/value list where “Fully Scouted” sits in green among the ratings — the player always knows whether he is reading a fact or a guess.

Playball's board is already honest about being vague. This is the interface vocabulary for saying so out loud.

The offseason is a wizard with a visible position

Both competitors do the same thing: a hero card in the team's colour, an “Offseason Timeline · Stage 1 of 4” progress bar, the current step and the next one as cards, one primary action. The offseason is where every dynasty game loses people, and everyone has landed on the same answer.

What players say is broken

  • Depth spread thin reads as depth removed. FM26 Touch redistributed dense screens across more screens; veterans call it exasperating and say vital screens got harder to find. Fewer taps beats fewer things per screen.
  • Accidental swipe-dismiss. An interactive dismiss on a screen holding unsaved decisions is a bug with a gesture.
  • Comparison that requires navigation. OOTP Go has no filter depth, so comparing two players means opening two profiles — in a game that is entirely about comparing players.
  • Settings that do not stick. Same app resets lineups and depth charts after every game.
  • Small type, and no pause. Full Court Rivals' two commonest complaints.

Playball found the same class of fault from the other side: absolutely-positioned layers inside iOS momentum scrollers produced five separately-reported bugs from one cause. The lesson generalises. On a phone, the container is the bug.

Part four · What keeps people playing

The loop is recruiting, and everyone knows it

A Football Coach: College Dynasty player describes the whole game as deciding, week by week, whom to pitch, whom to chase hard, whom to give up on and whom to make a late run at — “essentially everything you do besides watching or coaching games.” That game is 95% positive across 1,481 Steam reviews. Recruiting carries because it is the only system where a decision made now pays off in two years and the payoff is a person you named.

Emergent narrative is the retention mechanic

Football Manager has no cutscenes. Its text is generated by a web of triggers responding to context, and its players describe decade-long saves where a youth graduate becomes a club legend and then retires. The attachment is the product.

Playball already has more of this machinery than any mobile competitor: signature moments on a man's card, alumni whose professional careers play out and end, a career rivalry ledger, ninety-five named rival coaches with careers of their own, and a pecking order that genuinely moves — six of the top twelve programmes turn over across thirty seasons. The competition has none of it. CFB Simulator's answer is a social feed reacting to results; Astonishing's is forty random events.

The risk is the mirror image: a system nobody meets is a system that does not exist. Each of those needs a surface — a card, a line in an inbox, a page in the record book — or it is engineering we never get paid for.

One session shape, not two

The best line in the research is a player on Astonishing Baseball Manager: casual enough to pick up and put down at will, yet deep enough to play for hours. That is a single requirement — every screen must be safe to leave. State persisted, position restored, no modal that punishes a phone call. The counter-example is the PC port: OOTP Go and FM Touch are the deepest things on the platform and their reviews are about friction, because a game designed for a desk is being played in four-minute pieces on a bus.

The arc has to be legible, and the punishment fair

The two loudest complaints about the direct competitor are about progression, not features: prestige that barely moves after five winning seasons, and “lost my entire team after one down year despite consistent success.” College BBALL Coach 2 fires coaches after one year regardless of improvement, which makes the rebuild — the fantasy the entire genre sells — unplayable. Both are the same failure: told to build over a decade, judged on a one-season window.

Trust is a feature you can print on the listing

Three apps in this field market on it: no ads and no in-app purchases, no microtransactions and no gimmicks, offline, no ads, no account. And the most successful app's top review is a player shouting that it is not pay-to-play. This audience has been burned. Whatever the S+ player and Play Billing turn into, the listing should be able to say plainly what the money does and does not buy — and the game should be complete without it.

One more, cheap and repeatable: every review thread on the 4.8-rated competitor has a developer reply under it, including the one-star freeze report. The 3.7-rated app's eight-season save corruption has sat there long enough for several reviewers to find it independently.

Part five · What a 2026 phone expects

The second half of the brief: what makes an app feel bought rather than ported. Most of it is measurable.

BudgetTargetWhy it is that number
Process an input event≤ 50 msSo the response is visible inside 100 ms.
Visible response to a tap≤ 100 msBelow this, people read it as instant.
One animation frame16.7 ms60 Hz. At 120 Hz it is 8.3 ms.
Cold start, p50< 1.2 sOn the target device class, not a flagship.
Cold start, p95< 2 sFirst-session retention falls off past 2.5 s.
Play flags a cold start≥ 5 sAndroid vitals calls this excessive.

Every tap must paint something inside 100 ms even when the work behind it takes a second — which, for a game that simulates a season on a button press, means the button's own state change is the deliverable, not the result.

Motion that reads as physical

Material 3 Expressive replaced duration-based animation with a spring — stiffness and damping ratio instead of milliseconds and a curve. Not fashion: a spring can be retargeted mid-flight and the physics solves a new path, which is exactly what a gesture needs when the user changes their mind halfway through a drag. Duration curves cannot do that; they snap.

Playball's motion today is duration and cubic-bezier, and it is well chosen — the 220 ms sheet rise, the 260 ms directional bracket swap, one prefers-reduced-motion block that turns all of it off. That is right for everything that is not gesture-driven. The moment anything becomes draggable it needs a spring, or letting go mid-gesture will look wrong in a way nobody can name.

And the cheapest large win available: the View Transition API is supported in modern Android WebView, and same-document transitions — the SPA case, which is all of Playball — have been there since Chrome 111. Today .screen-in gives every screen a 260 ms rise, which is good and is also a fade in disguise: the outgoing screen is simply gone. A view transition carries a player's row into his card, or a crest from a schedule row into a scoreboard. That is the difference between navigating and moving.

The mechanical checklist

Touch, scroll, gesture

  • Targets ≥ 44–48 px. Three quarters of phone touches are a thumb; about half of all use is one-handed.
  • Primary actions in the bottom third. The top corners need a grip change.
  • Five bottom tabs maximum, then it collapses into More.
  • overscroll-behavior: contain on scrollers, or a pull inside a sheet drags the page behind it.
  • -webkit-tap-highlight-color: transparent plus a real pressed state, or every tap flashes a grey rectangle that says “web page”.
  • Never an absolutely-positioned layer inside a momentum scroller. Five bugs, one cause, already paid for.

Frames and lists

  • Animate transform and opacity only. Anything else is layout or paint, every frame, on the main thread.
  • will-change is a promise you pay for: set it on what is about to move, remove it after.
  • A stable 60 beats an unstable 90–120; requestAnimationFrame in a WebView is commonly still capped at 60 anyway.
  • Virtualise past ~100 rows; content-visibility: auto is the one-line version.
  • Put the recruiting board's filter behind React 19's useTransition so the input keeps taking keystrokes while results lag a beat.
  • Hardware acceleration on in the manifest.

The Android platform work

  • Target API 36. Play's deadline for updates to target Android 16 landed at the end of August 2026 — it is behind us.
  • Edge-to-edge is mandatory and no longer opt-out. Android 16 removed the escape hatch; the WebView draws behind the system bars.
  • Note that WebView below 140 returns wrong env(safe-area-inset-*) values; Capacitor 8.3.2+ exposes what you need to work around it.
  • Predictive back (Android 13+): enableOnBackInvokedCallback, and a handler that can show the destination mid-swipe. A back gesture that exits the app from the middle of a recruiting session is the worst bug on this platform.
  • Persist on visibilitychange, not on a timer. Backgrounded WebViews get killed.

Feel, sound, first run

  • Haptics with restraint — meaningful interactions only, immediate, and consistent enough to become vocabulary. A walk-off must not share a buzz with the back button.
  • Assume muted. Most mobile play is silent, so sound may never be the only channel for a state change.
  • Skeletons over spinners where the layout is known: a skeleton hands the brain the shape that is coming.
  • Optimistic UI for anything already known — a lineup, a watchlist, a coaching point. Show it, reconcile after.
  • Onboarding by doing, one mechanic at a time, visible milestones, a skip that works. A reviewer of the biggest app in the genre spent money on training without understanding what it did, and said so in the review.

Part six · The scorecard

Already ahead of the field

  • Reliability. 1,061 tests, determinism goldens, calibration as regression, a concurrency suite. Nothing in the genre is close — and reliability is the genre's defining failure.
  • The world. Ninety-five rival coaches with careers, realignment, a rivalry ledger, alumni pro careers, a pecking order that moves. The competition has a social feed.
  • Motion. Sheets, screens, directional swaps and one reduced-motion switch — better than every native list app in the field.
  • Voice. Stage 15.5 exists at all. Their play-by-play is one sentence per event; ours is meant to be a code worth learning.
  • Chrome. A bottom nav that reports a live number under each label is the best navigation idea in this study, and it is ours.
  • Safe areas, done before the port. Right order.

Gaps this study surfaced

  • No view transitions. Screens arrive; nothing travels.
  • Duration-based motion only. Fine today, wrong the moment anything is draggable.
  • No virtualisation on lists that are only going to get longer.
  • Save durability under process death is unproven — the genre's signature bug, and the one place the suite does not reach.
  • No onboarding, against a growing surface of systems.
  • Predictive back unhandled, because there is no Android project yet.
  • The dynasty's memory has thin surfaces. It all exists in the engine. How often does a player meet it?
  • No fast path through the depth. Every successful app here gives a competent default and a way to skip.

Part seven · In this order

  1. Prove the save survives a kill

    Persist on visibilitychange; write the test that interrupts a write and reopens. This is the moat — verify it rather than assume it.

  2. Build the fast path

    A coach's recommendation on every decision screen and a one-tap advance that respects it. Depth is not diminished by a default; it is made reachable in four minutes on a bus.

  3. View transitions

    Row to detail, crest to scoreboard. The cheapest thing on this list that changes how the whole app feels.

  4. Virtualise the long lists

    Or at minimum content-visibility: auto, and put the recruiting filter behind useTransition.

  5. Onboarding as a first season

    Not a tutorial. One mechanic per week of the opening schedule, skippable, taught by doing.

  6. Springs for anything draggable

    When dragging arrives — not before, and not for anything that is not gesture-driven.

  7. The Android platform pass

    Target 36, predictive back, edge-to-edge against WebView below 140, cold start measured at p50 and p95.

  8. Surface the memory

    Every system the player does not regularly meet is engineering we have not been paid for yet.
