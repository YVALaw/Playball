Playball · implementation plan · fifteen stages shipped

The road to v1.0

Nineteen stages, ordered by what unblocks what. The game is already deep; this is the work that turns it into a product with a personality, on a phone. The first fifteen are done. Three and four did not exist when this plan was written — they came out of playing what stage 1 shipped, and were built the same day they were reported. The old stage 5 was split so the dugout could land without a calibrated engine riding on the same pass — and both halves have now shipped, the second with the measurements to show what it cost. Stage seven, the coach, is complete — all eight pieces, ending with the press room — and stage eight, the roster, landed the same day with two-way players split out of it — with stage nine, players as people, behind it. The portal followed, and then the screen: a full mockup adopted as the design of record, every screen rebuilt on it, dark mode, and the app dressed in your school's own colours.

Two ways to play

The decision that shapes everything else

A player chooses how deep a game he wants, and the game respects it. Coach creation asks whether this is a full roleplay career or a casual one, and that answer presets everything the feature set adds — press conferences, academic eligibility, captains, pitch calling, mound visits, scouting reports.

Three rules keep that from becoming two games in one codebase, and they aren't negotiable:

  1. The engine always models everything. The mode changes what the player is asked about, never what the simulation does. Casual doesn't turn injuries off — it answers the injury question for you. The moment a mode reaches into the engine, the 95 rival programs live in a different world from yours and every comparison in the game is a lie.
  2. Anything that touches the world is on for everybody or off for everybody. Realignment, eligibility, injuries: properties of the league, not preferences.
  3. The preset is a preset, not a cage. Per-system toggles sit behind it, and the mode can change mid-career.

Two errands that need no phone

Create the Play Console record and merchant account. A web form that starts two clocks otherwise sitting at the end of the project: merchant verification takes days, and in-app products can't be tested until an app record exists.

Take a throttled performance profile in the browser. The open question the deferred phone work leaves is whether the 3D field holds a frame rate on mid-range hardware. 4× CPU throttle isn't the same measurement but it catches a disaster — and stage 5 rebuilds the dugout around a larger field.

  1. 01

    Stop the game lying, and stop it losing things

    Done · August 2026799 tests green
    • A13 — the elimination card that says your season is over when second place sends you to a regional. Wider than reported: a protected team losing its regional got the same funeral. Where you fell is now written at the moment you fall, because that's the only moment the bracket still knows.
    • Tournament win/lose cards — they carry the biggest moments in the game and are a title and two lines in a box. Cut to a headline and one line. The full big-moment treatment is stage 13 and wasn't worth doing twice.

Don't serialise the coroutine — persist the day-start snapshot and the ordered list of decisions, and replay. The engine is deterministic, so the replay lands on exactly the same sixth inning. The most player-hostile behaviour the game has on the platform it ships to. Built exactly that way, in the regular season and the postseason both — the second only after a save restriction that had quietly gone stale was deleted rather than worked around.
    Exit — metNothing the game says is false, and nothing a phone call can destroy.

  3. 03

    June, made legible

    Done · August 2026Reported and built the same day

    The postseason was rebuilt, then played. The verdict is worth stating precisely, because it decides what gets touched: the format is not the complaint. Double elimination, the regionals and the showdown brackets all did what they were built to do. What fails is the screen's account of them.

    • Cut the opening round. Its real sin turned out not to be the extra games: it was a single-elimination gate standing in front of a double elimination tournament. You could win your conference, win your regional, lose one series and be finished, in an event whose whole promise is that one bad night doesn't end you. Those eight teams now play their way in inside the winners bracket, where losing drops you to the losers side. Two ten-team halves; eighteen games each, nineteen with the reset — arithmetic rather than a chosen number, since nine teams must go out at two losses apiece.
    • A modal for every title game. Conference, regional, national — the biggest game of a stage should come to you, not sit in a slot you have to know to open.
    • A card for the champion. The winner was a thin stripe at the foot of the page, and the national title sat so far down it was missed entirely. A card at the top now, at three intensities from a conference banner to the country — one component, so the escalation itself tells you what you won. A rival's title gets the same card in navy and no takeover.
    • Tap a bracket game to see it. Line score, the pitchers, the swing of it. The box scores already exist and are already reopenable — this is routing, not new data.
    • Postseason statistics. There was no way to see them at all. June is counted a second time rather than moved — season totals include tournament play, so it couldn't be got by subtraction — and kept for all ninety-six programs, with career totals that survive the year roll.
    • Say so when a season really is over. The other half of A13: stage 1 stopped the false funerals, this stops the silence for a team that missed the conference final and won't reach the national field.
    • Less text on the June cards. The out-of-the-showdown card is liked and still over-written.
    Exit — metA player can read his own June at a glance, and knows the moment it ends.
    What the re-measurement saidThe cut was checked against the soak rather than assumed neutral, and it moved nothing: nine or ten distinct champions in thirty years either way, measured across five worlds. The concentration is the format, not the opening round, and it stays an open question rather than something quietly tuned away. The soak's own canary had been set at exactly the first number it printed, so it was firing on noise; it is a fifth of the years now, which catches a collapse instead of the weather.

It's a row of the frame now: measured across all six offseason tabs, two pixels from the bottom every time.

  5. 05

    The dugout

    LargeHighest value of anything leftNo engine risk

    Split out of the old stage 5 deliberately: everything here is presentation over a stream the engine already emits, so the best screen in the game could land without putting a calibrated simulation at risk in the same pass. What reaches into the engine is stage 6.

    • The presentation rebuild. A field about twice the size, batter and pitcher as cards carrying the three numbers you would actually want, an arm gauge drawing the pitch budget fatigue has always used — the first time that budget has ever been visible — outs as pips, and the inning with a direction caret. Corrected twice on a phone. The linescore was briefly folded behind a LINE SCORE button and came straight back out onto the bar with R/H/E: it is the one thing here that answers “where are we” without being asked, and a scoreboard you have to press is not a scoreboard.
    • Where everybody is, in the play log. It began as a dark banner over the foot of the field and was reported twice — once for covering both cards, once for existing at all. The second note is the better one: the diamond already shows the runners, so a caption over it is the same fact twice, and the place a reader looks for words about the situation is the log. It sits pinned at the top of it, because it is the state now rather than a thing that happened. The first report had a real cause too — an earlier edit had nested both cards inside the field block, so bottom: 0 resolved below them.
    • The bench coach takes it, two ways. Watch hands him the calls with a beat between them, so the field animates and you simply see the game. Auto drops the beat and stops the instant something worth managing arrives — a man in scoring position, a close game gone late, or an arm past its budget. Deliberately those three and no more: a handover that fires every half inning is one nobody uses. Neither changes an outcome; he submits the same default call the screen already highlights. One bug the types allowed: scoring position was written as a null check over three booleans, and false !== null is true, so every empty diamond counted as a man on second and the handover fired on the first pitch and handed straight back. TypeScript does not flag a comparison that is always true. Caught only by playing it.
    • June moved with it. The stage rail became navigation, so a tournament already decided can be reopened instead of ceasing to exist. The bracket view now follows the side you are playing on and fades between halves, because losing in the winners bracket used to leave you looking at the half you had just left. SIM TO MY NEXT GAME is the primary action with the named round beside it — round by round is the honest unit and is kept, but it is not what anybody wants when four of the next five rounds hold none of their games. And two colour faults: every championship card was one muted green, so three tournaments looked identical — bronze, silver and gold now — and finishing runners up wore that same green, which congratulated a team on losing its final. Green is for a trophy, clay for a season that is over, neutral for “you lost and you are still alive”.
    Exit — metThe dugout is the best screen in the game.
    Deferred, by requestThe ballpark’s look — crowd, stands, lighting, the texture of the place — as opposed to its geometry, which is done. It waits for broadcast, where sound and celebration land, so the park gets treated once and properly rather than twice by halves. REPLAY is also unbuilt; the events that would drive it are already stored and take zero random draws, so it can land any time.
  6. 06

    The dugout’s depth

    Done · August 2026First stage to touch the calibrated engine

    The half of the old stage 5 that reaches into the engine — split off so a tuning problem here could never hold up a screen that was already finished. It got the measured treatment fatigue got: isolate the channel, measure it against the calibration sweep, dial it.

    • Confidence, beside fatigue. They are a pair and the design keeps them apart: fatigue is a budget that only ever spends, confidence a state that moves both ways on what has just happened to him. ARM and HEAD, drawn together. The property that let a new channel into a calibrated engine: level confidence multiplies by exactly 1, so nothing moves at the midpoint and every figure measured before this still describes the same game. Its authority is a fraction of fatigue's — six percent at the extreme against forty-five. A man who has lost the plate should be worse, not a different pitcher.
    • Mound visits. One per pitcher per outing, resetting on a change because a new man has his own. Confidence only, never fatigue — talk is not rest, and letting it be would collapse two channels into one. All ninety-six programs use theirs, late and with somebody on. That symmetry is not a nicety: a settling mechanic available only to the human would put the rest of the country in a measurably different world.
    • What it cost, measured twice. The first sweep was taken before the AI visits existed and was therefore a measurement of half the change. Final: every component moved under one percent, every D1 target still passes. And the number most at risk — title concentration, since steadying good pitchers could plausibly cut upsets — came back at a mean of 9.4 champions across five worlds, against 9.4 before confidence existed. Unchanged.
    • Three tests that were asking the wrong question. All three failed because the world moved under a pinned seed. The batting champion was a ceiling against one season, which is the one thing a tail statistic cannot be judged on — the pinned seed threw .544 while nine others averaged .464 — so it judges the distribution across six worlds now. Widening the bound would have hidden a real regression later; picking a kinder seed is that with extra steps.
    Exit — metA pitcher’s outing has a shape, and you can do something about it.
    Revised on contact, and the tuning nearly went out wrongThe bars now start full and drain — a bar that fills as a man tires reads as something being earned, and both of these are things a pitcher arrives with and spends. But centring the multiplier on full, which is the obvious reading, taxed every arm in the country for the crime of having pitched: 1.2% on average, which run scoring being the nonlinear thing it is arrived as 4.9% more runs and put the league above target for the first time in this project. Neutral belongs where pitchers actually live, not where they start — walked down against the sweep to 0.76, which puts runs on target exactly and lets a cruising pitcher be genuinely worth about a percent. Confidence also feels traffic now, charged per runner left on.
    Deferred to stage 7: the conversationThe visit ships as a single confirm. The designed version — settle him, challenge him, take it off him, read against a pitcher's temperament, with repetition that costs — waits for the coach, because the half that makes it a decision is which register works, and that should read your personality badges as much as his temperament. Building it first means building the interesting half twice; the upgrade costs one sheet.
    Cut from v1.0: pitch-by-pitch callingNot deferred — dropped, and the reason is worth keeping. The engine resolves a plate appearance with log5 and then sequences pitches backwards to land on that outcome, so a pitch you called could not change anything already decided. Making the call a real input means a new calibrated channel for a full-depth-only feature; running a different engine for managed games breaks the rule that managing must not change the odds, and would make every record slightly dishonest; leaving it as theatre is the one thing this game does not do. The eleven pitch types stay what they are — what colours the play-by-play, and what tendencies and scouting are made of.
  7. 07

    The coach

    Shipped — all eight piecesVery large875 tests green

    The last part of the game that was still a form, and the stage where a coaching career stops being something that merely happens to you.

    Every fault below typechecked, passed the suite, and would have shipped. Five were found by measuring a distribution rather than reading the code — and three of those were the same mistake: the majority outcome being treated as an exception.

    • 1 · Ninety-six programmes that believe something. A name, a creed in their own voice, the one thing they are known for, and two dials. Hand-written, because deriving culture from prestige would have produced prestige wearing a hat. The test asks exactly that — and the first hand-written pass failed it. I had written the derived version by hand without noticing. Mobile Bay would rather tell you about 1974 than reach Omaha; Newport Bay have waited forty years and are prepared to wait longer.
    • 2 · Five questions at creation, drawn from eighty. An answer moves the four skills by net +2, negatives allowed — the single rule that keeps it a character question, because if one answer were worth +3 there would be a correct answer. Badges are a vote, not an award: each answer nominates one and the two most nominated are worn, so five answers leaning the same way make a coach who is obviously one thing and five that scatter make one who is harder to summarise. Which is also true of people.
    • 3 · A desk built in two halves. Three seats for programmes that specifically want you, two for the best jobs you could get regardless. One weighted sort could never work: weighted hard, every offer matched and the market read as a search result; weighted lightly, the same five best jobs rang for everybody. The band a rookie can reach is eight points of prestige across twenty schools, so a single dial can favour standing or fit and never both. Now eleven of twenty offers match your strongest leaning, and a recruiting school at prestige 32 will outbid better programmes for a man who said he was a closer.
    • 4 · Thirteen shapes a career can take. Journeyman used to mean has coached one game, which is why seventy-one of ninety-six wore it at year thirty. Measured twice. Six programmes is unreachable — the carousel's observed maximum over thirty-five years is four. A regional banner is not rare — June hangs sixteen a year, so "two" was the seventy-fifth percentile wearing a contender's name. And the first pass reproduced the exact fault it was fixing: the fallback was still Journeyman, so sixty of ninety-six wore it and none of them had moved anywhere. Most coaches have a long career and win nothing decisive, and that deserves its own name.
    • 5 · Writing to a school that has not asked for you. On its own page in Colleges, where you would actually go looking. Three a season, never the same school twice, no odds shown. Culture decides how it goes in both directions, which is what makes it a decision: a programme that prizes tradition is roughly twice as likely to mention it to somebody, so the schools most worth approaching are often the ones most likely to talk. Interest and risk roll separately, which is what allows the worst case — they want you, and they told somebody.
    • 6 · Ten hidden counters with seeded thresholds. Nobody farms a target they cannot see, and the same style earns its badges at different moments in two careers — one decision doing two jobs. Two of them reward engaging with the game rather than optimising it: reading the wire, and talking a man out of the draft. Its test greps the source for a write to each counter, crudely and on purpose. It exists because Builder shipped one piece earlier as a title nobody could wear — the number behind it was recorded for the player and for none of the ninety-five rivals.
    • 7 · Culture reaches the simulation, twice too strongly. Development only, and two edges — the one place a school's identity changes an outcome without changing a game. The calibration sweep did not move at all, structurally: the effect lives between seasons rather than inside one. But six percent compounds — paired on the same seeds, champion diversity fell from 9.5 to 8.25. Halved, it is indistinguishable from nothing. And drift was a ratchet: missing the postseason cost patience, but four in five schools miss every year, so the whole country slid toward twitchiness and turnover rose five percent. A board becomes less patient when it has just sacked somebody — nine schools a year, not seventy-six.
    • 8 · Twenty questions the season earns — removed September 2. Cut whole on the second test pass ("the press questions, we will remove that entirely"): engine, data, screen, store slice and the NEEDS YOU card went together, and old saves still load. What follows is the record of what it was. Nine triggers, eight a season behind a four-game cooldown — and every trigger is a fact the season already produced: a streak it counts, a result it recorded, a bracket it settled. Nothing is measured specially to feed this, which is what keeps a press conference a consequence rather than a scheduled event. This is what the personality badges were built for. An answer belongs to a badge; wearing it makes the answer land, because the room has heard you say this sort of thing before, and not wearing it costs a little, because the same words out of a stranger's mouth are a pose. The screen says SOUNDS LIKE YOU rather than printing a number — a press conference with "+1 PRESTIGE" beside each line stops being something you read and becomes a column you optimise. The lean applied to both channels first, and that was a gate rather than a lean: half a point rounds a neutral channel to a whole one in each direction, so an answer worth one and nothing swung by three on a badge. It lands on his name alone now, which is also truer — whether a man sounded like himself is a question about his reputation; the board is judging what he said.

    Then it got played — two full seasons, sixteen reports, eleven fixed the same day. The four worth keeping are about the shape of a mistake rather than one line of it.

    • A state flag with no way back is a wiped save. The program page stopped showing the college and showed the coach instead, permanently. programSheet moved into the store so an inbox card could address the page. The coach sheet renders its own frame with no tabs, and the back bar belongs to the overlay — so dismissing the overlay left the flag set, and the PROGRAM tab had no exit at all. The lesson isn't "add a back button": any state that outlives the screen which set it needs an exit on every screen that reads it.
    • Two definitions of one colour, twelve lines apart. "Red cards still read as a loss" — after the trophy colours had supposedly fixed exactly that. --clay is commented as "the accent: active states, rules, alerts" and, in the block added for the trophies, as the colour those cards had to be kept away from because it reads as a loss. Both were deliberate and neither is wrong. The collision was invisible until it painted your own row in every bracket. Identity has its own ink now.
    • A flag read one step before it becomes true. A man talked out of the draft was inducted to the hall of fame. The ballot excludes whoever is on a roster — the right definition, read at the one moment it is stale, because reinstate puts him back during the draft step and the ballot runs on leaving it. Fixed by reading the statement instead of its consequence: a draft outcome of stayed already is "still in the league".
    • A number calibrated for one role, applied to another. The opposing bench changed pitchers once a game while its reliever was "depleted and being hit around". The hook's budget is 30 + stamina, fitted to a Friday starter. Applied to a 35-stamina reliever it allowed sixty pitches before the flat twelve and a patient hook policy were added on top — ninety, a number a college reliever never reaches. Six earned runs is not "hit around" either; that is a disaster already finished.
    The one balance change, measured rather than arguedReaching the regionals paid nothing — madeTournament is a seat in the twenty-team field, so the thirty-two who lose a regional scored what a program that stayed home scored. The first fix — worth 5, with reputation falling slower than it rises for everybody — took the league mean from 54.1 to 61.1, dropped the spread from 17.7 to 14.6 and emptied the bottom star bucket entirely. Ninety-six programmes all drifting upward is not a league with a top to it. Worth 2, with the slow fall confined to programmes already above 70, lands at 56.5 and keeps the bottom tier.
    Exit — reachedTwo coaches with the same record are visibly different men, the job market is somewhere you can act rather than only be acted upon, and a second dynasty does not ask you the same questions as the first. One thing is still owed rather than missing: the cold letter is a real gamble at 18% rather than 12%, but nothing in the game acknowledges having written one until the carousel. Morale was the third channel the press was meant to move and it is deliberately absent — there is no morale system until stage 8, and wiring an answer to a number that does not exist is how Builder shipped as a title nobody could wear.
    Two the play report asked for, both smaller than they sounded"The five questions are too long." Measuring found the questions were never the long part — the asks have a median of nineteen characters ("Who hears about it?"). The setups had a median of 107 and ran to 163: three lines of scene before a one-line question, five screens running. All eighty are one line now, median 63, and pinned at 90 by a test, because this is the kind of thing that grows back a clause at a time. "The winners→losers transition is quite wild." The comment above the code that moves the view had claimed "the map fades in under it" since the day it was written, and the only transition in the file was a button background. There was no fade. There is one now.
  8. 08

    The roster becomes a roster

    Shipped — two-way split outLarge942 tests green

    Five things, four engine modules and two screens, resting on one decision repeated four times: everything new is derived or sparse, so a save from before it has nobody failing, nobody sitting, nobody settling and no chart — rather than everybody. Save growth is unchanged at 12.3 KB a year over thirty seasons.

    • The spectrum is the model. DH — 1B — LF — RF — 3B — CF — 2B — SS — C, free downhill and dear uphill, with catching carrying a surcharge on top. Secondary positions are derived from the ladder rather than generated, because a new field at generation moves every draw after it. And catching had to come off the ladder in both directions. Read as one ladder, a spectrum that puts catching at the hard end says every other spot is downhill from it — so the chart offered a catcher as free cover at shortstop. It is at that end because it is the hardest position to fill, not because catchers are the best athletes; they are usually the slowest men in the building. Found by looking at the screen, which no test would have done.
    • The chart, and the incumbent who leads it. A ranking per position and a lineup card are different facts, so the chart owns who plays where and the lineup keeps owning who bats when. Three bugs, and the third is the one worth keeping: ranked purely on merit it re-picked 94 of 96 lineups on day one. Correct in baseball terms — a good bench middle infielder really is a better left fielder than a weak corner outfielder — and ruinous, because the lineup is what the simulation plays. The incumbent leads his own spot now, so the chart's day-one answer is the card the generator already wrote and it differs only when somebody cannot play. Which is the entire job.
    • The classroom, which is the most college thing here. Your program only; ninety-five other rosters losing shortstops to a lecture is a slower roll and a bigger save to model what nobody can see. Visible and manageable rather than a hidden roll — a number you could have seen and ignored is a decision you got wrong, a hidden roll is a punishment. It shipped with failing unreachable: the distribution floored at 34 while the threshold was 28, so nobody in the country could be in the state the feature exists for. Third time for that shape. And it ran in the wrong place — off a hook that fires once after a whole season, so SIM SEASON checked one week after every game had been played. It lives in the day loop now.
    • Redshirts, by the real rule. Stricter than football's: no four-game grace, one appearance burns the season, so it is all or nothing. Freshmen and sophomores, once in a career, three a season. He develops at 0.85 — a redshirt who came back better than the man who played would make sitting everybody correct — and the decision it creates is the good one: your promising freshman is behind a senior, so play him now or have him when the senior is gone.
    • Moving a man is instant on the card with a settling penalty that decays over two seasons, and uphill moves settle harder. Not position training: a man is here two to four years, and spending one teaching him second base spends most of what you have.
    Split out, on the reasoning that split stage 5Two-way players, and with them declining the DH — assigning the slot ships, but letting the pitcher hit needs the same thing two-way does: the batting order is a list of hitters and a pitcher has no hitting ratings at all. A toggle for it was built, wired and put on screen before that was noticed, then removed — a control that changes nothing is the exact fault the previous stage spent a week deleting. One man in two rating systems with fatigue crossing both, a lineup card and a rotation both claiming him, and every leaderboard deciding which half of him it is ranking. Not a small feature in a big hat, and this stage was already large. They will arrive two-way rather than be made, and they will be as rare as they are in life.
    Exit — reachedYou manage a roster rather than reading a list. What it still wants is morale, which stage 9 brings — the press room is waiting for it too.
    LargeUnblocks 09, 10 and part of 11
    • Depth chart with position eligibility — a competence per position, not a boolean
    • Real DH handling — the coach assigns the slot
    • Redshirts, position changes and position-change training
    • Academic eligibility — a man fails a class and sits. Uniquely college, and it makes recruiting a kid with questions a real decision.
    • Two-way players — deferred for years and the decision has aged badly. Genuinely hard: one man in two rating systems with fatigue crossing both.

  9. 09

    Players as people

    ShippedLarge984 tests green

    Injuries, workload, morale, the promise and the captain — the stage that gives a season attrition and a clubhouse that notices. Same discipline as the two before it: everything derived or sparse, so a save from before it has nobody hurt, tired, unhappy or leading.

    • Injuries are league-wide, which grades are not. The opposite call to the classroom and for the opposite reason: nobody can see another program's grades, but a rival losing his ace is visible, it changes the team you are about to play, and a league where only your program breaks down is lying to you. Asked for as pure chance with no durability rating — which makes where the roll comes from matter more rather than less, because a hidden roll a reload could re-roll is a slot machine. Derived from the man, the day, the year and the world, so it takes no draw and cannot be re-rolled. About one man a program a year, mostly a weekend, season-enders about once every three springs.
    • Two bugs one game could not have shown. A roster that ran thin handed back a card with eight men on it — unreachable until injuries went league-wide, then reported as "Dubuque River Riverboats has an empty lineup slot". A program with five men down runs somebody out there who should not be out there; he plays hurt, and the cost lands on the coach who ran out of players. And a man was hurt on the same day every year of his career: the roll hashed the day index, which restarts every spring, against a seed that never changes. Chronic injuries, the same men, for ever.
    • Workload is slight on the bat and not slight on the odds. Three percent off the bat at the very floor; two and a half times the injury risk. That asymmetry is the design, and it came straight from the brief — "very slight, we don't want the roster dead after a few games". The cost of running a man into the ground is that he gets hurt, not that he forgets how to hit.
    • Morale moves performance and transfer risk, not development. A man who is unhappy does not get worse at baseball; he plays a little below himself and starts looking around. The expectation is stated rather than inferred, which is what makes it a promise you can break — recruiting a man on the offer of a job and then sitting him is a thing you did. A broken promise costs about twice what a kept one pays: a man given more than promised is pleased, a man given far less is aggrieved, and it is the second that walks out of the door.
    • One captain, gated on makeup. The gate is the whole feature. Without it, naming a captain is a free buff on your best player and the answer is the same man every year; with it the question is who in this room is actually like that — on the roster this was verified against, one man of twenty-three qualified. He damps swings in both directions and makes nobody happy, which is the distinction: a captain is not a morale bonus, he is the reason a bad April does not become a bad year.
    A method note, against myselfThe year-in-the-hash bug was found while chasing a drop in champion diversity — a figure that across the same session read 14, then 16, then 13 distinct winners in thirty-five years. It is noise, and it diagnosed nothing. The bug was real on its own terms and would have been just as real if every measurement had come back clean. Worth recording because the finding was right and the reasoning was not.
    Exit — reachedA season has attrition and a clubhouse that notices. Stage 10 has a head start: morale already computes a flight risk that nothing reads yet, written now because the mood driving it is modelled now — a number bolted on later against a shipped system is the more expensive order.

    The screen

    LargeShipped · August 30–31 2026

    Not a new system. A pass over what is already built, because a play report that was two thirds interface is the game telling you where it actually is: “we need to completely change the UI though, things are way too mashed together or not very clear, confusing etc.”

    The economy, and the staff it pays for

    Shipped · August 31 20261034 tests green

    Recruiting budget rebalance · player swaying as a real negotiation · facilities and budget upgrades. Every dollar should have at least two things it could have been.

    Why they moved hereBecause of what they cost. Paying for staff out of the program’s money — decided over prestige, and over the recruiting pool — is what makes hiring a hitting coach a real argument with the facilities and the board. That argument is the whole point of an economy, and it is why staff should not ship first on a budget invented for them alone.
    How it shippedOne annual budget in dollars, derived from prestige, three claims on it — wages, facilities, the scouting desk — and everything derived, never drawn: the market’s candidates, a poaching, a wage are hashes of stable facts, so a reload cannot reroll a better applicant and no golden moved. Assistants stack on the coach’s own skills through the channels the skills already use; a rival’s staff stays priced in, the same fiction casual mode has always told. The scouting desk closed the free window: an opponent’s tendencies, readable for nothing since the tendency system landed, are now a bought book — cheap per report, ruinous as a habit. Poaching is derived per man per winter and the inbox names him when he goes. Salary is annual; poaching only at the roll; a poached coordinator misses the next class, which is exactly what he costs.

The order, and why

Stage 2 is foundational. Every feature after it needs a documented answer for what it does in casual mode, and retrofitting that answer is far more expensive than writing it as you go.

Stages 8 → 9 → 10 are a chain and can't be reordered. A depth chart makes injuries possible; injuries and playing time make morale mean something; morale is what gives the portal its teeth.

September 1 — a thirty-season session, and what it found. One long sitting produced more defects than any deliberate audit has, and three were bugs rather than balance: the per-game stat maps were keyed by player name, so two men sharing one shared a stat line; AUTO could not bench an injured man because the helper it calls is a pure reorder by contract and nothing sat above it; and availability was never asked on any pitcher path, so a suspended Friday starter started Friday. Three quarters of every recruiting class carried the wrong growth curve, the player’s board was the only one in the world not corrected for league drift, and the number every walk-on is drawn against had been welded down since world creation. All fixed and measured. Position fit then reached the simulation — and landed without a single re-calibration, because a sound nine assigns to itself.

Stages 12, 13 and 14 are independent of everything above and of each other — the ones to move earlier when the big systems get heavy. Stage 14 in particular will feel like a bigger jump than its size suggests, because silence is the loudest thing about the game right now.

Stages 3 and 4 were cheap relative to what they fixed, and both existed because the game got played rather than reasoned about. Stage 1 shipped a postseason, an evening with it produced thirteen findings, none of them things a plan would have predicted, and all thirteen were built the same day they were reported. That is the argument for playing the thing early and often.

One rule came out of that evening and is now policy, because it generated more than half of stage 3: a card is a visual telling of where you are and what you achieved, in simple wording. A card does not explain. Whatever needs explaining belongs in a tutorial, a tooltip, or nowhere.

Considered and not taken: a human poll alongside RPI, weather and park conditions, fan support and attendance, live bracketology, mentorship pairs, defensive positioning, exhibition games, classic-finish scenarios, share cards. All stay in the backlog with the reason attached — a rejected idea with a reason is worth more than a forgotten one.
